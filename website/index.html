The connection

Audio moves through a familiar Mac device.

Choose AES67 Mac in your audio app. The driver carries audio across your Ethernet network to a compatible AES67 stream, and brings incoming streams back to CoreAudio.

01

Pick the audio device

Your DAW, playback app, or recorder sees a CoreAudio device with separately configurable input and output channels.

02

Configure the stream

In AES67 Manager, select Ethernet, discover streams, choose a channel profile, and apply your configuration.

03

Route at the other end

Set up the peer’s AES67 stream, multicast route, and clock settings on the network equipment itself.

Before you begin

Made for AES67 interoperability.

This project speaks AES67 RTP audio. It does not implement native Dante audio, discovery, or device control.

You need

An Ethernet interface, an AES67-capable peer, compatible stream and PTP settings, and administrator access for installation.

For Dante-equipped devices

Enable AES67 interoperability on the device first. Routing and subscriptions remain specific to its card, firmware, and network setup.

Get started

From download to first signal.

Start with a known test signal. Verify each direction separately before using both at once.

1

Download and verify

Get the installer package and SHA256SUMS.txt from the current GitHub release. Compare the package’s SHA-256 checksum before opening it.

2

Install, then restart

The installer is unsigned and the included programs are not notarized. Follow the macOS first-open steps in the guide. Install outside an active audio session; installation restarts CoreAudio. macOS 11 is the deployment target, but runtime compatibility varies by setup.

3

Connect and test

Choose the Ethernet interface and stream in AES67 Manager, select the device in your audio app, then check PTP lock, channel mapping, and audio at both ends.

Field notes

Tested in real setups. Check yours carefully.

Recording and playback have been exercised with a Behringer X32 and X-DANTE. Mac-to-console playback on a Yamaha TF5 AES67-capable setup has also been reported; its card and test conditions are undocumented, and the reverse direction has not been tested.

Hardware tests have found occasional audio-buffer drops and PTP-lock interruptions. A lost PTP lock pauses transmission. Keep another path available for critical recording or playback.
